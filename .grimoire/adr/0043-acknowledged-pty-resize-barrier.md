# Acknowledged PTY resize barrier

**Status:** Proposed
**Date:** 2026-09-21

## Context

Harbor's terminal owns a blocking PTY reader thread that publishes byte chunks to the UI thread through a bounded channel. ADR-0042 requires terminal resize to prepare model state, resize the PTY, and commit the prepared model transactionally. Draining the channel once before preparation does not establish an ordering boundary: a read may complete concurrently, and its chunk may be published after the drain but before resize commit. That chunk would then be parsed against the new model geometry even though the reader completed it on the pre-resize side of the transaction.

Repeated drains cannot close this race because they do not prove that the reader has stopped publishing. Waiting without a way to interrupt a blocking read could stall the UI indefinitely. Dropping or duplicating a chunk while interrupting the reader would corrupt the terminal byte stream.

## Decision

Extend the synchronous PTY ownership established by ADR-0013 with an acknowledged, epoch-qualified resize barrier shared by the terminal reader and PTY control capabilities.

Before resize preparation, the UI requests a barrier for a unique epoch. The platform PTY implementation must make a blocked read interruptible for this protocol. The reader publishes every byte from any read that completed before it acknowledges that epoch, then publishes the acknowledgement and starts no further read until explicitly resumed. Successfully returned bytes are delivered exactly once even when a barrier request races with read completion.

The UI waits only for a bounded interval. After receiving the matching acknowledgement, it drains and parses all preceding chunks under the old terminal geometry. It keeps the reader paused while it prepares model state, resizes the PTY, and commits the prepared model, then resumes the reader. Bytes read after resume belong to the post-resize side of the boundary and are parsed under the new geometry.

If barrier acquisition, PTY resize, or model preparation fails, the terminal leaves PTY and model geometry unchanged wherever the existing transaction has not already crossed its PTY commit point, resumes the reader, records the failure, and permits a later resize retry. Barrier timeout must not leave the reader paused or allow an unacknowledged resize to proceed. Resume is required on every success and error path.

The barrier contract orders reader-observed byte chunks; it does not claim to timestamp when the child process generated bytes that remain unread inside an operating-system pipe.

## Consequences

- A one-time opportunistic channel drain is not sufficient for resize correctness.
- `harbor-pty` must provide platform-specific interruption and acknowledgement while keeping raw platform handles behind its capability boundary.
- `harbor-terminal` must represent data and barrier acknowledgements in one ordered reader event stream or an equivalently provable protocol.
- Resize may wait briefly for acknowledgement, but the wait is bounded and failure preserves the prior geometry for retry.
- Tests must force both sides of the race: a completed read delayed before publication and a reader blocked when the barrier is requested.
- Tests must prove no chunk loss, duplication, publication while paused, stale acknowledgement reuse, or reader leak across success, timeout, PTY failure, terminal drop, and retry.
- Windows ConPTY runtime evidence remains required because deterministic channel tests cannot validate platform read interruption.
